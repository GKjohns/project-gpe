OpenAI papers explorer — storyboard

Pivot of Project GPE from an operator tool into a public reading site for the openai/math collection: 721 manuscripts in 373 result families across 17 disciplines, written by an unnamed internal model and released as "raw research data". Our product is the layer that makes one of them readable: a proof map, named nodes with plain-English statements, novel-vs-routine classification, the PDF linked to the map, and a chat that cites nodes. The catalog (every family, every abstract) is complete on day one at zero model cost; deep digests are built progressively, starting with the five already done. Everything about ingesting, runs, review and cost moves behind the existing operator login. The explorer internals do not change; frames 05–06 are copied from the v1 storyboard to say so.

How a paper reads here

The source PDFs are dense and unsigned. The explorer gives each one three handles.

01

The map

Every theorem, lemma and in-proof claim becomes a node; "uses" edges are drawn as a layered DAG with the main result on top. Click a node and its whole support lights up.

02

The node

Each step has a name, the paper's statement, a plain-English version, why it is here, and a classification: novel, routine, or a known result with its citation.

The Exponential Time Hypothesis is False

We give a deterministic algorithm that counts the satisfying assignments of any k-CNF formula on n variables in time 2n/(log n)1/2+o(1), refuting the Exponential Time Hypothesis. The algorithm reduces #k-SAT to counting common zeros of sparse low-degree polynomial systems over 𝔽2, and counts those by fixing a small design of linear measurements that factors the sum. Consequences include subexponential MAX-SAT approximation and new circuit lower bounds via the algorithms-to-lower-bounds connection.

The Exponential Time Hypothesis is False
Oct 25, 2026·67 pp·main_full_version.tex·Digested
We show that for every fixed k ≥ 2 there is a deterministic algorithm that, given a k-CNF formula with n variables and m clauses, outputs the number of satisfying assignments in time (n+m)Ok(1) · 2n/(log n)1/2+o(1) and polynomial space. The core is a counting routine for common zeros of sparse polynomial systems whose running time is a product of three exponential terms, each controlled by a different parameter of an explicit combinatorial layout, together with a deterministic sparsification and a greedy choice of parity hashes certified by the counter itself. Appendix D removes a log log n loss at fixed clause density via Boolean tensor contraction.

The Exponential Time Hypothesis is False

What this paper proves

It gives a deterministic algorithm that counts the satisfying assignments of any k-CNF formula faster than 2cn for every c > 0, which the Exponential Time Hypothesis says is impossible. The trick is to turn "count the solutions" into "count the common zeros of a sparse polynomial system", and then to count those by fixing a small set of linear measurements that splits one enormous sum into many independent small ones. Balancing the three costs that fall out of that split is where the √log n in the exponent comes from.

Main theorem

theoremnovel Main theorem: subexponential exact #k-SAT thm:main · p. 2
You can count exactly how many assignments satisfy a k-CNF in time that grows slower than any 2cn. That contradicts the Exponential Time Hypothesis, which says 3-SAT needs 2Ω(n) time.
Theorem 1.1. For every fixed k ≥ 2 there is a deterministic algorithm that, given a k-CNF formula with n variables and m clauses, outputs the number of satisfying assignments in time (n+m)Ok(1) · 2n/(log n)1/2+o(1) and space polynomial in n + m.

Where the new idea lives · 5 novel steps

Lem 3.2
Fixing measurements splits the sum
One 2n-term sum becomes a product of small sums, one per variable group.
Prop 3.4
Common counting routine
Running time is 2τ·2n/r·2D; three costs, three knobs.
Lem 4.7
Parameter choice balancing the three costs
Set the three equal and the exponent lands at n log log n/√log n.
Lem 6.2
Greedy choice of parity hashes
The counter certifies each hash, which is what makes the algorithm deterministic.
Thm D.1
Boolean tensor contraction
Removes the log log n loss at fixed clause density.

Counterexamples to infinite matroid intersection and packing/covering
Sep 30, 2026·21 pp
The infinite matroid intersection conjecture of Nash-Williams and the packing/covering conjecture of Bowler and Carmesin have been open since the modern axiomatization of infinite matroids. We give explicit counterexamples to both. The matroids are constructed from a rooted tree of countable branching whose independence relation is determined by a finite-to-one labelling; we show that every pair of finite minors admits the required intersection while the full structure does not, using a diagonalization against candidate common independent sets.

Open questions

  1. Scope of digests. Curate ~20 flagship families first (the five done plus the headline TCS and geometry results), or crawl all 721? At the careful profile's $5–25 per paper, 721 papers is roughly $4k–18k and weeks of wallclock; 20 families (~40 manuscripts) is $200–1,000 and a weekend. Proposal: flagship first, "Request a map" tallies demand.
  2. Product name. "GPE" is a placeholder. Needs a name that reads as a reading room, not a dev tool. Not "Proof Atlas" (taken).
  3. Human-written fixtures. The arXiv papers already in the DB (pfr, sensitivity, sunflowers) are not in openai/math. Keep as hidden admin fixtures (public=false, no family), or delete?
  4. Hero image. Direction is a classical oil painting of a mathematician at a desk; candidates in hero/README.md. Pick one, or shoot a different direction (engraving, plate from a 19th-century treatise).
